01 — About
From Stage to Launchpad

I started my academic journey studying cello performance at Interlochen Arts Academy, one of the nation's premier arts boarding schools. As a musician, I learned that excellence requires relentless practice, collaboration, and thoughtful compromise — every ensemble member contributes to a greater whole.

That same mindset now drives my work in aerospace engineering at Rutgers University. Switching from music to engineering meant adapting to completely different ways of thinking, but it taught me how to quickly absorb new concepts, persist through complex challenges, and apply creative thinking to technical problems.

Today I'm building a flapping wing drone from scratch, designing mechanical systems, and serving as treasurer for a campus organization — always learning, always building.

Designing and building a flapping wing drone from the ground up — wing and drive-linkage geometry modeled in Onshape, flapping kinematics and actuator response simulated in MATLAB and Simulink, and an onboard microcontroller driving the flapping mechanism and flight control.

Designed a steel bear canister exploring multiple geometries and incorporating industry-standard hardware. Used PrePoMax with custom meshing parameters for accurate simulation under various loading conditions, optimizing for weight and structural integrity.
